Each ceramic piece follows a careful journey from raw clay to finished, functional pottery. Here's how my pieces come to life in the studio.

## 1. Clay preparation

I work primarily with stoneware clay, chosen for its strength and workability. The clay is wedged to remove air bubbles and prepare it for throwing.

- High-quality stoneware clay body
- Wedging removes air bubbles that could cause cracking
- Clay is prepared in batches for consistency

## 2. Wheel throwing

Most of my pieces begin on the potter's wheel, where the clay is centred and shaped through the rhythm of spinning and gentle pressure.

- Clay is centred on the wheel head
- Walls are pulled up slowly and evenly
- Forms are shaped through gentle pressure and tool work
- Each piece keeps the organic qualities of hand throwing

## 3. Trimming & finishing

Once leather-hard, pieces are trimmed to refine their form and create foot rings for stability.

- Excess clay is removed from the base
- Foot rings are carved for proper sitting
- Surface textures may be added at this stage
- Final form adjustments are made

## 4. First firing (bisque)

Dried pieces undergo their first firing in the electric kiln to transform clay into ceramic.

- Temperature: approximately 1000°C
- Duration: 12–16 hours including heating and cooling
- Creates porous ceramic ready for glazing
- Pieces become much stronger but still absorbent

## 5. Glazing

After bisque firing, pieces are carefully glazed using techniques that enhance both beauty and functionality.

- Pieces are cleaned and prepared
- Glazes are applied by dipping, brushing, or pouring
- Multiple layers may be used for depth and variation
- All glazes are food-safe and lead-free

## 6. Glaze firing

The final transformation happens in the glaze firing, reaching stoneware temperatures for strength and durability.

- Temperature: cone 10 (approximately 1280°C)
- Duration: 14–18 hours total cycle
- Glazes mature and become glassy
- Clay and glaze fuse completely

## 7. Final inspection

Each piece is carefully inspected for quality before leaving the studio.

- Visual inspection for glaze defects
- Testing for functionality (pouring, stability)
- Minor imperfections that add character are preserved
- Only pieces meeting my standards are offered

---

The whole journey from clay to finished piece typically takes 2–3 weeks, most of that spent in careful drying and firing cycles.
